# Visual Style Catalog — Directions, Not Templates

This catalog helps name visual directions. It does not authorize copying a brand or applying one style to every product. Choose a direction only after understanding product, audience, content, and constraints.

## Editorial / typographic

**Use when:** the brand depends on ideas, writing, culture, expertise, or a strong point of view.

**Build with:** expressive but controlled type scale, intentional line breaks, generous measure, editorial grids, strong image selection, varied section rhythm.

**Avoid:** oversized type that pushes useful content below the fold, random font mixing, decorative quotation marks, and empty space used to hide weak content.

## Product-led / interface-forward

**Use when:** users need to understand a software workflow or product capability.

**Build with:** real interface states, realistic data, clear navigation, precise labels, diagrams that explain actual flows, restrained surfaces.

**Avoid:** fake app screenshots, unreadable mock UI, decorative browser chrome, and features that do not exist.

## Technical / information-dense

**Use when:** the product serves developers, operators, analysts, or expert users.

**Build with:** compact but readable typography, aligned values, clear hierarchy, deliberate density, keyboard affordances, contextual detail, precise status semantics.

**Avoid:** making every label monospace, reducing contrast to look “terminal-like”, hiding actions behind hover, and using charts as decoration.

## Warm / human / hospitality

**Use when:** the experience depends on place, people, atmosphere, care, or service.

**Build with:** real photography, warm but legible palette, useful practical details, natural language, clear booking/contact paths.

**Avoid:** generic gradients, fake staff photography, unrelated lifestyle stock, and burying opening hours/location.

## Minimal / quiet

**Use when:** the product needs calm, focus, and low cognitive load.

**Build with:** disciplined type, restrained palette, whitespace that groups content, few visible actions, careful alignment.

**Avoid:** minimalism that removes labels, hides navigation, lowers contrast, or creates unexplained blank areas.

## Expressive / playful

**Use when:** the product audience and task benefit from personality and delight.

**Build with:** distinctive type or illustration, a controlled accent palette, meaningful interaction feedback, clear hierarchy, and a consistent voice.

**Avoid:** childish tone for serious tasks, noisy motion, excessive badges, and personality that obscures function.

## Luxury / premium

**Use when:** the product has real material, service, craft, scarcity, or brand reasons for restraint and detail.

**Build with:** strong photography/material representation, precise type, confident spacing, restrained controls, transparent service information.

**Avoid:** black backgrounds and gold accents as a substitute for quality, tiny low-contrast text, and inaccessible minimal controls.

## Data / operations

**Use when:** users need to monitor, compare, diagnose, or act on changing information.

**Build with:** clear scope and time range, strong tabular alignment, meaningful status colors, exception-first hierarchy, useful filters, drill-down.

**Avoid:** vanity KPI tiles, unlabeled charts, false precision, random gradients, and hiding freshness or data provenance.

## How to choose

Score candidate directions against product fit, content fit, audience expectations, accessibility, implementation cost, and maintainability. Pick one dominant direction and at most one supporting influence. Do not mix unrelated styles merely to make a page “unique”.

## Direction statement template

“Because [audience] needs to [job] in [context], the interface should feel [two or three traits]. We will express this through [type/layout/color/media/interaction], and deliberately avoid [two anti-patterns].”
